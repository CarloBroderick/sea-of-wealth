The question at the heart of the book

Should we halt the use of the ocean’s resources—or learn to use them better?

Humanity needs food, clean energy, transport, minerals, and ways to manage carbon. The ocean may help provide all five. A Sea of Wealth asks what each opportunity offers, what it risks, and how we can make choices that work for people and the planet.

About the book

A hopeful argument for a thriving blue economy.

As governments and companies rush toward the blue expanse covering two-thirds of our planet, Ben Halpern asks not simply whether we should use the ocean—but how.

Blending cutting-edge science with personal stories from a life spent in and around the sea, Halpern travels the globe to investigate food, energy, transport, minerals, and carbon. He weighs their potential against their environmental risks and compares them with the choices we already make on land.

The result is a clear-eyed, surprising case for approaching the ocean not as an untouched wilderness or an unlimited storehouse, but as a living system whose future depends on wiser decisions now.

About the author

Ben Halpern

Marine biologist, conservation scientist, and one of the world’s leading thinkers on the relationship between people and the ocean.

Ben Halpern is professor of marine biology and conservation science at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and executive director of the National Center for Ecological Analysis and Synthesis. His research has appeared in Nature, Science, and Scientific American, and his work is regularly featured by NPR, the BBC, and other major media outlets.
